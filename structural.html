RC pipes: a standard range designed to code

ASTM C76M Classes III to V, AASHTO LRFD indirect and direct design

A pipe range must carry earth, traffic and water loads in four installation types, prove its class in the three-edge-bearing test, and stay consistent across every size, class and cage.

How it works

An order line names a diameter, a class and an installation. The engine takes the cage from the standard range, checks it by indirect and direct design, and writes the memo, the drawing set and the bar bending schedule for that exact product.

What I built

  • Design engine for ASTM C76M Classes III/IV/V to AASHTO LRFD 2024 §12.10: indirect design with installation Types 1-4, and direct (Heger) design on a closed-ring frame.
  • Loads: earth, fluid, HL-93 and MOMRA vehicles with live-load distribution, dry and submerged states. Checks: flexure, crack control, diagonal tension, radial tension, steel limits, stirrups, flotation.
  • Verified against Eriksson Pipe and a SAP2000 ring model; three-edge-bearing proof testing to ASTM C497 written into the inspection and test plan.
  • Outputs per product: indirect and direct memos, a Revit drawing set, BBS and an 86-page catalogue.

Manholes: circular and rectangular, as shell models

AASHTO LRFD 10th, ASTM C478M and MOMRA live load in SAP2000

Buried manholes carry earth, traffic, groundwater and handling loads through stacked pieces whose joints take compression only; covers carry eccentric openings, and every depth type needs its own design.

How it works

The control workbook takes the depth, openings and loads. The engine builds a SAP2000 shell model for every piece, runs it through the API, reads every result back and writes the memo with each check traced to its reading.

What I built

  • Each precast piece modelled as a SAP2000 shell with compression-only joints, bedding and base springs; loads cascade from the cover down to the base.
  • Loads: earth with ACPA arching, at-rest pressure on effective stress, HL-93 with MOMRA surcharge, groundwater with buoyant soil weight, handling with a dynamic factor.
  • Checks: biaxial flexure on every face, hoop compression and tension, shear, strut-and-tie and punching at cover openings, crack control, flotation at every joint plane, bearing, joint sliding and lifting.
  • Slabs designed by Wood-Armer; every printed check traced to its SAP2000 reading in a 44-54-page memo.

Box culverts: a frame on Winkler soil

AASHTO LRFD 10th or MOMRA, ASTM C1433M / C1577, ACI 318M-25

A box culvert's design depends on fill, vehicle and soil stiffness together; single and twin cells, two code routes and the handling of heavy units all have to be covered.

How it works

Single or twin cell, AASHTO or MOMRA: the designer builds the frame on compression-only springs in SAP2000, accepts the soil contact only after equilibrium and complementarity checks, runs every vehicle placement, then issues the memo, compliance matrix, bar schedules, shop drawings and lifting design.

What I built

  • Plane frame with haunches on compression-only Winkler springs, run in SAP2000 through its API; a soil-contact solution is accepted only after equilibrium and complementarity checks.
  • Loads: earth with Marston and trench factors, lateral earth, surcharge, water, and HL-93, HS20 or MOMRA vehicles with dynamic allowance; every generated vehicle placement evaluated.
  • Checks: flexure with thrust, shear, crack control, minimum steel, wall stability, bearing, flotation and lifting at each handling stage.
  • Package: memo, clause-by-clause compliance matrix, BBS, shop drawings and lifting design, verified by an independent frame, an independent contact checker and a truth set of published cases.

Jacking pipes for microtunnelling

BS EN 1916 and DWA-A 161: ring, jacking force, joints, end zones and fatigue

Jacking pipes are pushed through the ground: besides earth and traffic they carry the jacking force through a deflected joint, and the end zones and collars decide the capacity.

How it works

The range is defined by its moulds. For each size the engine declares the jacking force at the design joint angle, checks the ring, joints, end zones and collar, verifies them in SAP2000 and generates the Revit set.

What I built

  • Design basis: BS EN 1916 and BS 5911-1 with the DWA-A 161 static calculation, ATV-DVWK-A 127 soil loads and EN 1992-1-1 section checks.
  • Declared jacking force as the lower of EN 1916 and DWA-A 161 at the design joint angle, with packer stiffness; ring flexure and crack width, fatigue, end-zone splitting and padding ties, collar checks.
  • SAP2000 verification: ring frame, a 3D solid model of the pipe end at the collar, and the steel can of the intermediate jacking station for stress and buckling.
  • A range of 21 moulds with two cages, 50 and 100-year design lives, lined and unlined.
  • Revit drawing sets per size: general arrangement, joint with an exploded 3D half section, 3D cages and BBS; intermediate jacking station sets for the male and female units.

Steel moulds for jacking pipes, drawn in BIM

A Revit mould set for every size: parts, casting assemblies, details and design checks

Each pipe size needs its own steel mould: a jacket, a collapsible core, a pallet and a header that cast the joint geometry exactly, carry the fresh-concrete pressure under vibration and hold the product tolerances cast after cast.

How it works

Each mould set is generated from the same product data as the pipe it casts, so the joint geometry on the mould and on the pipe drawing come from one source.

What I built

  • A mould set generated per size from the same product data as the pipe: cover with the parts in 3D, casting assemblies, pallet, jacket halves, collapsible core, header, intermediate jacking station units and a seal-zone gauge.
  • Design checks per part: fresh-concrete pressure as a full liquid head with a factor of 1.5 for vibration, lifting lugs to EN 1993-1-8, and the governing ratio printed for every part.
  • Fabrication rules on every set: S275JR steel to BS EN 10025, welding to BS EN ISO 3834-3 and ISO 5817, mould tolerances derived from BS EN 1916 Table G.3, and shop checks before the first cast.
  • Automated drawing guards hold every sheet to the drafting standard before it is issued.
